A rule reading `neither` mostly cannot fire on this data rather than failing to. The paired corpora are plain prose with no markdown, no headings, and no emoji, so the structural rules (`inline-header-bullet`, `bold-overuse`, `emoji-decoration`, `heading-dependent-opener`, `demonstrative-heading`, `horizontal-rule`) and the glyph rules have nothing to match. Judge those on the false-positive table below and on the unit fixtures, not here.

Two rules score BELOW 1, meaning they fire more on the human side: `ellipsis` and `reversed-antithesis`. Both are already off by default, and this is the second independent reason for that. `invisible-unicode` is near zero on generated text because model output is typographically clean; it catches a provenance problem (watermarks, fingerprints, injection) rather than an authorship tell, which is why it is always on and scored separately.

Caveats that travel with every number above:

- Vintage. The generators are GPT-3 curie, ChatGPT 3.5, and Claude, all 2022 and 2023. Current models write differently, and several tells in this linter postdate these datasets. Recall measured here is a FLOOR.
- Register. Encyclopedic intros, Q&A answers, student essays, news, and creative writing, rather than the landing copy, launch posts, and newsletters where slop is thickest and where this linter is actually pointed. Marketing prose would score higher.
- Prompting. These were generated by plain completion prompts, not by someone asking for an engaging blog post, so the social-post tells (engagement bait, reveal framing, puffery) have little chance to appear.
- RAID (liamdugan/raid) was evaluated and rejected as a source: its rows are grouped by adversarial attack, including a zero_width_space perturbation that would corrupt the invisible-unicode measurement, and its filter index was unavailable across repeated attempts.
